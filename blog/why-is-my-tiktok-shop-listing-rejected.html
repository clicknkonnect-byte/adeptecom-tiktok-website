Why is my TikTok Shop listing rejected?

The real reasons TikTok Shop rejects US listings, how to read the rejection reason TikTok actually gives you, and the fastest path to getting a compliant listing live.

A rejected listing is one of the most common — and most avoidable — problems US TikTok Shop sellers run into. It's rarely random: TikTok Shop's review system checks a listing against a fixed set of documentation, category, and content rules, and a rejection almost always traces back to one of a handful of causes. This guide walks through what those causes are and exactly how to fix them.

1. Why was my TikTok Shop listing rejected?

Most TikTok Shop listing rejections come down to three things: missing or incomplete documentation, a listing placed on the wrong category leaf, or attribute and marketing-claim issues that violate TikTok's product policies. The rejection notice TikTok sends usually names one of these directly, so the first step is always reading that reason carefully rather than guessing and resubmitting blind.

2. What documentation issues cause rejections?

Documentation is the single biggest cause of rejected listings, especially in regulated categories. Cosmetics, supplements, and household chemicals typically require a Safety Data Sheet (SDS); supplements often need a certificate of analysis; and every seller needs valid business and brand-authorization documents on file. A listing gets rejected when the document is missing, expired, or doesn't match the product's category and claims exactly — for example, an SDS that lists different ingredients than the listing describes. If your account is new or was set up quickly, this is the first place to check; see Setup & API Onboarding for how a documentation and compliance pack should be built before launch.

3. Why do category and attribute mismatches cause rejections?

A listing placed on the wrong category leaf gets rejected because TikTok evaluates it against that category's specific rules, and a mismatch between the product and its category is treated as inaccurate or misleading. This often happens when a seller picks the closest-sounding category instead of the most specific correct one, or leaves required attribute fields (size, quantity, material, skin type) blank or vague. Attributes aren't just for search ranking — TikTok's review system uses them to check policy compliance, so incomplete attribute data can trigger a rejection even when the product itself is fully compliant.

4. Why do images, video, or claims get a listing rejected?

Listings also get rejected for content reasons: images with watermarks, contact information, or before/after claims; video that shows prohibited use cases; or copy that makes a medical, therapeutic, or "cure"-style claim TikTok doesn't allow outside a licensed category. This is especially common in beauty, health, and supplement listings, where sellers copy claims from packaging or Amazon listings without checking them against TikTok's advertising policies. The fix is to rewrite claims to describe what the product is and does, not what it treats or cures, and to remove any imagery that references pricing, contact details, or other platforms.

5. How do you fix and resubmit a rejected TikTok Shop listing?

Start by reading the exact rejection reason in Seller Center rather than assuming the cause, since documentation, category, and content issues each need a different fix. Correct the specific field or document flagged, keep the category and attributes consistent with that fix, and resubmit — submitting the same listing again without changing anything simply repeats the rejection. If a listing has been rejected more than once, it's worth checking the account's overall category and document status, since a deeper account-level compliance issue can cause repeated rejections across multiple products.

6. How long does TikTok Shop take to re-review a resubmission?

Most resubmissions are reviewed within one to three business days, though regulated categories such as health, beauty, and supplements can take longer. A resubmission that still has an incomplete document or an unresolved claim issue triggers another rejection and restarts the review clock, which is why it pays to fix the root cause completely before resubmitting rather than making a quick partial edit.
