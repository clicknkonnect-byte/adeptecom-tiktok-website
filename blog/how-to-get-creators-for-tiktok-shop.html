How to get creators for your TikTok Shop

A practical playbook for US sellers who need real creators posting real content — not just a program switched on with nobody picking up the product.

Turning on the Affiliate Program is the easy part. The hard part is getting creators to actually notice your product, request a sample, and post content that sells — and that comes down to a handful of decisions most sellers get wrong on the first try. Here's how we approach it for the US brands we manage.

1. Make your listing worth a creator's time

Creators check your product page before they request a sample, so a thin listing with weak photos and no highlights gets skipped even at a strong commission. Fix the listing first: clear product shots on a plain background, honest claims backed by real attributes, and highlights that answer "why would I film this" before you chase a single creator. A creator who lands on a listing with one blurry photo and no product story assumes the brand hasn't invested in the basics, and moves on to the next request in their inbox. See Listing Optimisation for how we approach this.

2. Set a commission that actually competes

Your commission is competing against every other brand asking for the same creator's time, so price it against attention, not a round number. Most US sellers start around 10-20% on Open Collaboration for reach, then add a flat fee or higher rate on Target Plan invites for creators with proven conversion. Price it against your margin rather than copying a competitor's number outright — a low-margin consumable needs a different structure than a high-ticket gadget, and a rate that looks generous on paper can still be too thin to move a creator who has dozens of other offers open.

3. Combine Open Collaboration with direct invites

Open Collaboration alone brings volume but uneven quality; direct Target Plan invites bring the creators you actually want, but take more legwork to find and pitch. Run both — publish a competitive open rate for reach, and personally invite a shortlist of creators whose audience and content style match your product. Search TikTok itself for creators already posting in your category, check that their engagement looks real rather than inflated, and reach out with a specific, low-friction offer instead of a generic template.

4. Approve samples fast, and screen for fit over follower count

Speed beats perfection on sample approvals — same-day responses keep creators from moving on to a faster brand. Screen for content style and audience fit in your category first, follower count second; a smaller creator whose audience matches your buyer often outsells a bigger one who doesn't. Set clear, written approval criteria up front so decisions aren't made ad hoc, and budget realistically for product cost plus US shipping on every sample you send — some won't convert into content, and that's a normal cost of building the pipeline, not a failure.

5. Brief for native content, not a scripted ad

A short brief covering the one problem your product solves, a hook that's already worked, and what to avoid outperforms a rigid script every time. Creative freedom inside a tight brief is what makes content look native to the platform instead of an obvious ad, and native content is what actually converts on TikTok Shop. Keep the "avoid" list short and specific — overclaiming, competitor mentions, off-brand tone — and let the creator's own voice and format choices do the rest.

6. Build a real creator network instead of one-off outreach

One-off DMs to creators are slow and don't compound; a vetted, ongoing creator relationship does. Sourcing, vetting, sampling, and re-engaging creators at scale is exactly what a dedicated creator engine is built for — see the US & UK Creator Engine, built on a network of 10,000+ vetted creators. A recurring relationship also means a creator who converted well once can be re-briefed on a new product or promotion without starting the sourcing process from zero.

7. Track GMV per creator, then scale what already converts

Views and likes tell you a video was watched; GMV per creator and per video tells you it sold something, and those two lists rarely match. Once organic content is converting, put paid spend behind it with Spark Ads and GMV Max rather than testing untested creators with ad dollars — more in Ads, Spark & GMV Max. Review this weekly during a launch and monthly once the program is steady, since a creator mix that worked at launch can drift as new competitors enter your category.

Most brands that struggle to get creators aren't dealing with a platform problem — they're dealing with a handful of avoidable gaps: a listing too thin to convince anyone it's worth filming, a commission that can't compete for attention, slow sample turnaround, or a brief so rigid it produces stiff, obviously sponsored content. Fix those in order and creator pickup usually improves within the first couple of weeks, because the demand from creators for well-run programs is already there.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get creators for my TikTok Shop?
Enable the Affiliate Program on your listings, publish a competitive commission through Open Collaboration for reach, and directly invite proven creators through Target Plans for your best product-creator matches, backed by fast sample approvals and a clear brief.
How many creators do I need to launch a TikTok Shop?
Most US brands see traction from a first wave of 15-30 seeded creators; the goal isn't raw volume but finding the handful whose content actually converts, then doubling down on that mix.
Do I have to pay creators upfront to work with my TikTok Shop?
No. Standard TikTok Shop creator deals are commission-only on completed sales; upfront cost is typically just the product sample and US shipping, with flat fees reserved for Target Plan invites with top performers.
Why aren't creators picking up my TikTok Shop product?
The most common causes are a commission rate too low to compete for creator time, a thin product listing that doesn't convince creators it's worth filming, slow sample approvals, or a brief that's too rigid for creators to make native content.
