TikTok Shop returns and account health in 2026

What TikTok Shop's Account Health Rating actually tracks, how returns really factor in, and what to do before a falling score starts blocking listings or ads.

TikTok Shop moved US sellers to a new Account Health Rating in 2026, replacing the older violation-points system many sellers had gotten used to. At the same time, returns remain one of the most confusing parts of running a shop — publicly reported return-rate numbers for TikTok Shop swing wildly depending on the source. This guide pulls apart what account health actually tracks, where returns really fit in, and what to do before a dropping score starts limiting your shop.

What is TikTok Shop's Account Health Rating, and how does it work?

The Account Health Rating (AHR) is a 0-1,000 score, with new shops reported to start around 200 and the healthy band sitting at 200 and above. As the score falls, restrictions escalate; industry write-ups describe milestone actions around 150, 100, 50, and 0 points, with 0 deactivating the shop. Sources disagree on some mechanics — the scoring window is described as either the last 90 or 180 days depending on the write-up — so treat third-party guides as a map, not the rulebook. The actual deduction list for your account lives in Seller Center's Account Health page, and that's the source to check first.

What actually counts against your account health

Across the available guidance, a handful of triggers show up repeatedly: late dispatch and wrong tracking numbers, merchant-initiated cancellations, and a mismatch between what's listed and what's shipped. Misleading claims or promoting prohibited items on a listing or an ad can also cost you, as can intellectual-property complaints and promotional activity that pushes buyers off-platform. A violation on one linked account can carry over to another. None of this is exotic — it's mostly the same fulfillment and listing discipline that keeps a shop compliant in the first place. How returns affect your TikTok Shop account, beyond the AHR

Returns aren't always a direct line item inside the AHR itself. After-sales handling time and related measures typically live under a separate Shop Performance Score (reported on a 0-5 scale), which can affect things like affiliate access, campaign eligibility, and settlement speed. On the actual return-rate numbers, public sources disagree sharply — estimates range from a fraction of a percent up into the double digits, depending on category and how "return rate" is defined. Rather than benchmark against an industry average that may not match your category, track your own seller-fault return rate and its trend over time. One industry guide recommends keeping that figure under roughly 1.5% to protect your store rating and buyer trust.

How to actually reduce your TikTok Shop return rate

Several levers show up consistently in seller guidance. Creator content sets the expectation a buyer walks in with more than the product page does, so briefing creators on accurate claims matters — see how we run that through the US & UK Creator Engine. Beyond content, the basics still carry weight: clear photos and video, honest sizing detail, and packaging that actually survives transit. Reviewing return reasons on a regular cadence — not just the total count — surfaces the specific product, listing, or shipping issue driving them. It's also worth watching for a small number of customers who repeatedly abuse a return policy, since that skews the numbers without reflecting a real product problem.

What to do when your account health rating drops

Open the Account Health page in Seller Center and read each deduction with its reason, date, and appeal status rather than just the headline number. Correction windows, appeal deadlines, and compliance quizzes referenced in some deductions have real expiry dates — act inside them instead of waiting for the next milestone warning. If you're also running TikTok's native AI tools, remember they can flag an issue but shouldn't be the last check before a listing or campaign goes live; see our breakdown of TikTok Shop AI automation for where a person still needs to be in the loop.

What to ask a TikTok Shop agency about returns and account health

Account health is a good filter for whether an agency actually runs operations or just posts content. Ask how often they check your Account Health Rating and Shop Performance Score, and who owns the weekly review of return reasons. Ask how creator briefs are checked for claims that could drive returns, and what their plan is if your score approaches an enforcement threshold. A red flag worth noting: an agency that can talk at length about ad spend and creative but goes quiet when you ask about account health monitoring is usually a content shop wearing an operations label.
