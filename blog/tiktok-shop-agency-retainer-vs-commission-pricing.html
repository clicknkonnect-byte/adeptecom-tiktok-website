TikTok Shop agency retainer vs. commission pricing: which should you choose?

Two quotes for the same scope of work can look nothing alike once one agency bills a flat retainer and the other takes a cut of GMV. Here's how each model actually works, and which one fits where your brand is today.

Most US brands shopping for TikTok Shop help get quotes that don't line up: one agency proposes a flat monthly fee, another wants a percentage of sales, and a third blends the two. None of those structures is a scam — they're different ways of pricing the same underlying work, and each one shifts risk between you and the agency differently. This guide breaks down retainer, commission, and hybrid pricing so you can compare quotes on the same terms; for the fuller picture of what drives 2026 US pricing overall, see our guide on how much a TikTok Shop agency costs.

1. What's the actual difference between retainer and commission pricing?

A retainer is a fixed monthly fee for a defined scope of work, paid regardless of how much GMV the shop does that month; commission pricing ties the agency's pay directly to a percentage of the GMV they help generate. Retainer pricing gives you predictable costs and is easier to budget around, especially in the early months before sales volume is proven. Commission pricing aligns the agency's incentive with your sales outcome, but it means your cost rises and falls with revenue — and most agencies will only take commission-only deals once a shop is already doing meaningful monthly GMV, since the agency is effectively carrying more of the early-stage risk.

2. How much do TikTok Shop agency retainers typically cost?

Published agency pricing guides put flat retainers at roughly $3,000 to $10,000+ per month for mid-market scopes, with some full-service 2026 quotes for larger accounts reportedly reaching $40,000/month. The number alone tells you very little — what matters is what's bundled into it. A retainer that only covers ad management is a different product than one that covers setup and compliance, listing optimization, creator sourcing, Spark Ads, and LIVE selling. Before comparing two retainer quotes, get a written breakdown of exactly which services each dollar amount includes, and ask whether creator commissions, samples, and ad spend sit inside the retainer or on top of it — they're almost always on top. 3. How does commission-based (percentage-of-GMV) pricing work?

Commission pricing charges the agency a percentage of the GMV they drive, commonly cited in industry guides at roughly 10% to 20%, with some sources listing 15% to 25% for larger accounts. The appeal is obvious: the agency only earns more when you sell more, so there's no bill for a month where nothing moved. The catch is that commission-only arrangements are typically reserved for brands that can already prove a baseline of monthly GMV — agencies generally won't take on the staffing cost of running a shop from zero purely on a sales cut, because the downside risk is too lopsided in the brand's favor if the launch is slow. If you're pre-launch or early-stage, expect most serious agencies to start you on a retainer or hybrid rather than commission-only, and to revisit commission once volume is established.

4. What is hybrid pricing, and why do most agencies land there?

Hybrid pricing combines a base retainer that covers staff time and fixed costs with a smaller performance share that scales as GMV grows, and it's the structure most published agency pricing guides describe as the common middle ground for serious programs. The retainer protects the agency against a slow month and protects you from paying a large percentage on early, unproven sales; the performance component keeps the agency motivated once volume is real. A workable hybrid should have both parts stated in writing — the exact retainer, the exact percentage, and the exact GMV or revenue definition the percentage is calculated against (gross sales vs. net of refunds and platform fees matters a lot at scale).

5. What costs sit outside the agency fee, whichever model you pick?

Creator affiliate commissions, product samples, and TikTok ad spend are separate line items in nearly every pricing model, and skipping this question is the most common way buyers underestimate their real monthly cost. Creator commissions are commonly cited at roughly 10% to 30% of the order value depending on category and creator tier, samples and shipping add up when seeding at volume, and ad spend for Spark Ads or GMV Max campaigns is budgeted entirely separately from whatever you pay the agency. Ask any agency for a full monthly cost model — agency fee, expected creator payouts, sample budget, and recommended ad spend — not just the headline retainer or commission rate, so you can compare the true all-in cost across quotes.

6. Which pricing model fits your brand's current GMV stage?

Pre-launch and early-stage shops are generally best served by a flat retainer or a retainer-led hybrid, while established shops doing consistent monthly GMV can negotiate more commission weight into the mix. If you're setting up a US seller account for the first time, you need dedicated hours on compliance, listings, and initial creator seeding regardless of whether sales have started yet — a model that only pays the agency once GMV appears doesn't match that reality. Once a shop has a proven, repeatable sales pattern, shifting more of the fee into a performance share rewards the agency for continuing to grow it rather than just maintaining it. It's also worth noting that public reporting in 2026 described TikTok piloting its own in-house managed-service option for some US sellers — a flat monthly fee plus a per-sale commission that varies by category — which gives buyers a useful reference point when sanity-checking an independent agency's quote, though terms for any such program should be confirmed directly with TikTok rather than assumed from secondhand reporting.

7. Red flags in either pricing model

An unusually cheap retainer, a commission rate with no cap or minimum spend defined, or a contract that won't specify what counts toward GMV are the three most common pricing red flags. A retainer priced well below the published ranges above often means a junior account manager split across many clients rather than dedicated attention — ask directly who will be staffed on your account and how many other shops they handle. A commission structure should also state plainly whether it's calculated on gross sales, net of refunds, or net of platform and payment fees, since that distinction can change the effective rate significantly at scale.
