TikTok Shop agency flat fee vs. percentage of GMV: which costs you less?

Two agencies can quote wildly different numbers for the same scope of work once one bills a flat fee and the other takes a cut of GMV. Here's the actual math for figuring out which one is cheaper for your shop.

Comparing a flat-fee quote to a percentage-of-GMV quote by eye almost always leads to the wrong conclusion, because the two numbers aren't denominated in the same thing until you plug in your actual sales. This guide walks through the crossover math that tells you, for your own GMV, which model is genuinely cheaper — and why the answer tends to flip as a shop grows. For the broader pricing picture beyond just these two models, see our guide on retainer vs. commission pricing, and for overall 2026 US cost ranges, see how much a TikTok Shop agency costs.

1. Flat fee or percentage of GMV: what's actually being compared?

A flat fee is a fixed monthly cost set in advance regardless of how much the shop sells; a percentage-of-GMV fee rises and falls with sales, so it isn't fixed at all. The real comparison isn't which number looks smaller on a one-page quote — it's which structure costs less once you apply it to your own expected monthly GMV, because a small percentage can still outcost a larger-looking flat fee once sales pick up.

2. How do you find the GMV level where a percentage fee starts costing more?

Divide the flat-fee quote by the proposed percentage (as a decimal) to get the crossover GMV — below that GMV, the percentage fee is cheaper; above it, the flat fee is cheaper. For example, a $999/month flat fee compared against a 15% GMV fee crosses over at $999 ÷ 0.15 ≈ $6,660 in monthly GMV: sell less than that and the percentage model costs you less; sell more and the flat fee wins. Run the same division with each quote you receive — it's the single fastest way to compare two structurally different proposals on equal terms, and it only takes the flat-fee number and the percentage rate from each quote.

3. What percentage of GMV do TikTok Shop agencies typically charge?

Industry pricing guides commonly cite 10% to 20% of GMV for commission-only arrangements, with some sources listing as high as 25% to 30% for larger accounts or categories where creator costs are bundled into the rate. Where a quote lands in that range usually reflects scope (ad management only vs. full-funnel creator sourcing, listings, and LIVE) and account size, so ask explicitly what services the percentage is meant to cover before comparing it to another agency's rate.

5. Does the better model change as your GMV grows?

Yes — the crossover math means low-GMV shops are usually cheaper on a percentage fee, while shops reliably clearing the crossover GMV every month end up paying meaningfully more under a percentage than they would under a flat fee for the identical scope of work. This is exactly why fast-growing shops often renegotiate toward a flat fee or a hybrid with a capped performance share once volume is consistent: the agency's workload doesn't scale linearly with GMV the way an uncapped percentage fee does, so past a certain point the percentage stops reflecting the actual cost of the work. 6. What costs sit outside either pricing model?

Creator affiliate commissions, product samples, and TikTok ad spend are separate line items under a flat fee or a percentage fee alike, and this is the most common way buyers underestimate their true monthly cost. Creator commissions are commonly cited at roughly 15% to 30% of order value depending on category and creator tier — see our US & UK Creator Engine for how that's structured — and ad spend for Spark Ads or GMV Max campaigns, covered in our Ads, Spark & GMV Max service, is budgeted entirely separately from whichever agency fee you choose. Ask any agency for the full monthly cost model, not just the headline flat fee or percentage rate, so your crossover comparison reflects the true all-in cost.

7. What should be in the contract before you sign, whichever model you pick?

Get the exact GMV definition (gross sales vs. net of refunds and platform fees), any cap or minimum, and exactly what's bundled into the fee versus billed separately, all stated in writing. The GMV definition alone can shift the effective rate significantly at scale, and a percentage fee with no cap can become the more expensive option well past the crossover point you originally calculated. It's also worth noting that public reporting in 2026 described TikTok piloting its own in-house managed-service option for some US sellers — reportedly a flat fee around $10,000 plus a 10% to 20% per-sale commission depending on category — a useful reference point for sanity-checking an independent agency's blended quote, though exact terms should be confirmed directly with TikTok rather than assumed from secondhand reporting.
