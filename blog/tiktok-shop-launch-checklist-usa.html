The TikTok Shop launch checklist every US seller should run first

A step-by-step TikTok Shop launch checklist for US sellers — what to lock down before you upload a single SKU, why most new shops stall out, and how to tell in week one whether the launch actually worked.

Opening a TikTok Shop seller account in the US takes an afternoon. Getting it genuinely ready to sell takes a checklist most sellers skip. This guide walks through the five areas that decide whether a US TikTok Shop launch actually produces orders in week one — entity and banking, catalog and pricing, fulfillment, compliance, and creator readiness — plus what to watch once you're live.

What "launch ready" actually means on TikTok Shop

A TikTok Shop launch is ready when your entity and banking are locked, your catalog is priced for the full fee stack, your fulfillment plan can absorb a sudden spike, and a first batch of creator content is already queued — not simply when your seller account gets approved. That distinction matters more than it sounds: public reporting puts the number of registered US TikTok Shop seller accounts above 800,000 by mid-2026, and more than half of them have never recorded a single sale. Passing TikTok's own approval step confirms you're allowed to sell. It says nothing about whether you're actually set up to.

Entity, verification, and banking: the paperwork that stalls launches

TikTok Shop supports both an Individual Seller registration and a registered-business registration (sole proprietorship, LLC, or corporation), and the two paths ask for different documents and carry different category and payout limits. Decide which one fits before you start the application, not partway through it. The single most common delay we see isn't a missing document — it's a name or address that doesn't match exactly across your government ID, tax records, and the bank account you link for payouts. Confirm those three line up before you submit anything. Catalog and pricing: start narrow, and price for the real fee stack

Launch with one to three hero SKUs, not your full catalog. Pick products that are visually demonstrable in a short video and already have some proof of demand — an Amazon sales history, existing reviews, or a track record on another channel — rather than items that are hard to show or completely unproven. Before you set a price, run the real fee stack: TikTok's referral fee varies by category, and on top of it you'll set an affiliate commission to pay creators, which industry pricing guides put well above the referral fee alone in most categories. A listing priced only against the headline referral fee routinely turns out unprofitable once commission is added. Our TikTok Shop listing optimization work builds this fee math into pricing from day one instead of fixing it after the fact.

Fulfillment and inventory: plan for spiky demand, not steady demand

Amazon-style safety stock is built around steady, forecastable demand. TikTok Shop demand is spikier by nature — one creator video taking off overnight can outrun a replenishment cadence sized for a slower channel. Decide upfront between seller-fulfilled shipping, TikTok's own fulfillment programs, or a hybrid, and carry a separate inventory buffer for your TikTok Shop SKUs rather than assuming stock earmarked for another channel will cover a sudden spike. Treat fulfillment as something to settle before launch, not something to figure out the week a video goes viral.

Compliance, creators, and content: get sign-off before the first video goes up

Review TikTok Shop's category and claims policies before you upload listings, not after a creator posts something that gets your shop flagged — restricted and policy-sensitive categories typically need a second round of approval beyond the standard seller application. Once that's clear, line up your first wave of creator content: most seeding programs expect the large majority of individual sample sends to produce no direct sale at all, so treat early seeding as building a content pipeline, not judging a failed campaign. A small number of creators — often under ten — usually end up driving a disproportionate share of early GMV once that pipeline is running, which is why getting your first batch of content queued before launch matters more than seeding broadly after the fact. Our TikTok Shop affiliate agency work runs exactly this kind of pre-launch creator pipeline for US brands.

What to watch in your first two weeks live

Don't judge a launch on day-one GMV alone. Track your Account Health Rating and seller-fault return rate from day one — a rough first two weeks on either can throttle growth before sales even have a chance to build. Watch your content completion rate (the share of sample sends that actually turn into a posted video) rather than raw sample volume, and check margin per order once commission and fulfillment are counted, not just top-line sales. If you're bringing in outside help to run the launch, ask the same way: how they price for the real fee stack, who owns the account-health number, and what their plan is if a video spikes demand faster than fulfillment can absorb — a vague answer on any of those is a real warning sign, not a detail to sort out later.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do before I launch a TikTok Shop in the US?
Lock five things before you flip the switch: your entity and banking details match across ID, tax records, and your linked bank account; your catalog is priced for the real fee stack, not just the headline referral fee; your fulfillment plan can absorb a sudden spike from one viral video; any policy-sensitive category has its approvals in hand; and your first batch of creator content is already queued. Passing TikTok's seller approval only confirms you can open a shop, not that the shop is ready to sell.
How many SKUs should I launch with on TikTok Shop?
Most launch guides recommend starting with one to three hero SKUs rather than your full catalog — products that are visually demonstrable in a short video and already have some proof of demand, such as an Amazon sales history or existing reviews. A narrow launch is easier to track, easier to resupply if it spikes, and gives you a clean read on what's actually working before you add more SKUs.
Do I need an LLC to open a TikTok Shop in the US?
Not necessarily. TikTok Shop supports both an Individual Seller registration and a registered-business (Sole Proprietorship, LLC, or corporation) registration, each with a different document set. An individual account is faster to open but has lower limits on categories and payout options in most seller guides, and switching from individual to a registered business later adds friction — worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting to whichever path is fastest.
How do I know if my TikTok Shop launch actually worked?
Don't judge it on day-one GMV alone. In the first two weeks, watch your Account Health Rating and seller-fault return rate, how many of your sample sends to creators actually turned into a posted video (your content completion rate), and whether orders are tracking toward your margin target once commission and fulfillment are counted — not just the top-line sales number.
